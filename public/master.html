See what good looks like

Avoid: "A creative, structured professional with the ability to engage with customers in both effective and impactful ways."

Do this: "I'm a marketer who learned the trade running events for my mum's café. Twelve years on, I still believe the best campaigns start with knowing one customer properly. I work best in honest teams. My border collie thinks I should retire and walk her more."

From The Com'mon People CV guide. Specific, warm and memorable, and it sounds like a real person.

Skills you can prove

Name the skill, then one line that proves it: what you did, and what came of it. "Cash handling: cashed up the till every Saturday for a year" beats "good with money".

Stuck? Ideas for skills you can prove
  • Customer service: served 200 covers on a Saturday shift; regulars asked for me by name.
  • Cash handling: cashed up the till every closing shift for a year without a discrepancy.
  • Looking after people: cared for my grandad after his stroke, managing his medication and appointments.
  • Organising: planned our Year 11 prom for 120 students, including the budget and the DJ.
  • Leading a team: captain of the under-16s for two seasons; set up the training rota.
  • Digital skills: built and ran the Instagram page for my aunt's bakery.
  • Languages: fluent in Polish; translated for customers at my Saturday job.
  • Practical skills: rebuilt two bikes from parts and now fix friends' bikes.

Only write what's true for you. Small, real proof beats big, vague claims.

Experience

Latest first. Just tell us about it in your own words, typed or spoken, then press Turn this into CV points. Or write the points yourself.

Stuck? Ideas for what to say

What did you do? Served, cooked, cleaned, cashed up, greeted, answered, restocked, delivered, looked after, trained, organised, planned, fixed, built, sold, set up, ran, sorted, checked, helped new starters.

What came of it?

  • Something you were praised, thanked or trusted with ("Trusted to open up on my own after three months").
  • Something that got better because of you.
  • Numbers: money raised, people served, hours worked, days without missing a shift.
  • Anything new you learned or were given more responsibility for.

Reasons for leaving that read well: went back to college, moved house, a better opportunity, the job was seasonal, the contract ended, left to care for family. Still there? Leave it blank.

Education and training

Where, what, when and, if they're good, what you got. Certificates and short courses count too.

Stuck? Ideas for what counts
  • GCSEs: list your subjects and grades, or say "9 GCSEs including English and Maths".
  • A levels, BTECs, T Levels, NVQs and diplomas, with grades if you have them.
  • Apprenticeships and traineeships, finished or in progress.
  • Food Hygiene Level 2, First Aid, Manual Handling, Fire Marshal.
  • Driving licence, CSCS card, Personal Licence, forklift licence.
  • Duke of Edinburgh, Prince's Trust, National Citizen Service, Scouts or Guides awards.
  • Online courses you finished, such as Google, OpenLearn or FutureLearn.
  • Still studying? Put "Expected" and the date.

Interests

Underrated and powerful. Interests are how a stranger finds a reason to like you before you've met. Be specific ("netball for a local team" beats "sport") and be real. Three to six is plenty, one per line.

Tap an idea to add it, then make it yours: say which team, which games, which cause.

Your 90 seconds Optional

Built on Field Guide N°02. It works in four parts: who you are, two short stories from your work, and what you'd bring. We'll turn your CV into talking notes for each part. They're prompts to practise from, not a script to read out.

Plan my 90 seconds uses AI to turn what's in your CV into notes. It only uses what you've told us, and your name and contact details are taken out first. Anything it couldn't find is left as a question in brackets for you to fill in.

Practise it

Read your notes, then put them away and tell it out loud. Press start and the timer moves through the four parts. Three goes is the sweet spot.

Keeping yourself safe

  • Upload it to YouTube as Unlisted, not Public. Only people you give the link to can watch it.
  • Keep where you live and study out of shot: no school or college uniform or badge, no street signs, no front door or house number.
  • Say your name, but leave out your address, date of birth and anything personal the job doesn't need.
  • Only send the link through the employer's own application, or to an email address on their website. If someone you don't know messages you asking for a video, don't send one.
  • You can take it down whenever you like: delete it, or set it to Private.
  • Under 18? Watch it with a parent, carer, teacher or tutor before you send it. If anything online ever worries you, Childline is free and confidential on 0800 1111 or at childline.org.uk.
